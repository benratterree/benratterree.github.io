Autonomous Payload Delivery Drone

June 2026 – Present · In progress · Personal project

Overview

I built this drone from scratch using purchased parts and parts I designed and built using 3D printing/CAD. The drone can autonomously fly to a GPS waypoint, deliver a payload from a predetermined altitude, return to the launch location or another location and land on its own. It can deliver a payload of about 1 lb (a full 16 oz drink) with a flight time of ~15 minutes fully loaded. Autonomous takeoff, landing, and payload delivery all work. The next step of the project that I am currently working on is training an image-recognition model that will allow the drone to drop the target visually without GPS coordinates.

How it works

The drone is controlled by the flight computer, a Pixhawk PX4 which interfaces with a Raspberry Pi 4 that gives the flight computer commands such as waypoints to fly to and arming/disarming the drone. All of the wiring for the motors and power delivery to the Pi and flight computer was custom soldered to fit the drone's frame and all components were mounted to custom mount pieces I designed in CAD.

What I learned

The drone has had three catastrophic crashes where the drone was damaged. The first two crashes were caused by a compass error induced by high magnetic fields when the motors were in a high throttle transitional state (in this case on takeoff). After reviewing data from the first crash to try to characterize it I came to an incorrect conclusion because I thought that the drone was solely using GPS for control in the GPS autonomous mode. After the first crash I switched to dual GPS and when that didn't fix the issue (it crashed again) I looked at the data again and did more research and saw that the compass would give bad readouts almost a full second before the GPS location would change. The flight computer was using the compass in the flight computer for direction and, being much closer to the motors the interference was such that the compass would be as much as 270 degrees off. The solution ended up being calibrating the flight computer to adjust for the compass interference at different levels of current flow from the motors.
